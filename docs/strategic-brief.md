## 1. Executive Summary

Stream Kim Hicks Wrage & Alfaro is a Riverside-based civil litigation firm with more than a century of combined experience across regulated industries — public entities, healthcare, construction, real estate, and complex business disputes. The firm has the bench, the wins, and the institutional client base to operate at the same tier as nationally recognized litigation boutiques like Williams & Connolly. The current website does not reflect that reality.

This brief lays out a redesign that repositions Stream Kim as a serious civil litigation firm — confident, restrained, content-forward, and locally rooted in the Inland Empire. The visual and editorial register is borrowed from the elite-litigation tier (Williams & Connolly, Munger Tolles, Williams Lea), adapted for a regional firm with national-quality work. Every recommendation in this document is in service of one outcome: a prospective general counsel, public agency administrator, or institutional client lands on the new site and concludes within ten seconds that this is the firm to call.

### 1.1 Positioning Statement

> **Recommended positioning**
>
> Stream Kim is a Riverside civil trial firm representing institutions
> in their highest-stakes disputes. We defend public entities,
> healthcare providers, construction-industry clients, and businesses
> operating in regulated environments — and we have done so,
> successfully, for over a century of combined practice. When the
> matter is going to trial, we are who other lawyers refer.

This positioning is defensible because the underlying facts support it: long-tenured attorneys, regulated-industry depth, defense-side institutional clients, and a litigation-first practice mix. The site should communicate this without ever using the word "premier."

### 1.2 Strategic Goals

1.  Reposition Stream Kim at the elite civil-litigation tier through visual register, editorial voice, and content depth.

2.  Capture defensible local SEO surface for Inland Empire / Riverside / San Bernardino civil litigation queries.

3.  Build a content engine — practice insights, representative matters, attorney thought leadership — that sustains organic visibility and supports business development.

4.  Convert the right visitors: general counsel, risk managers, public agency administrators, healthcare administrators, brokers, and referring attorneys.

5.  Fix the credibility-eroding defects on the current site (broken stat block, thin practice pages, no proof) before redesigning around them.

## 2. Current State Audit

The existing site is built on what appears to be a generic WordPress legal theme. It has the right structure — homepage, about, attorneys, practice areas, contact — but every page reads like a template. The site does not communicate firm-specific positioning, attorney-specific credibility, or geographic/practice depth. Below are the nine concrete defects identified in the initial review, ordered by impact.

#### Critical defects

-   The homepage stat block displays "0+ Client Consultations / 0% Successful Cases / 0+ Professional Attorneys." This is a broken animated counter. It is currently live and signals technical neglect to every visitor. This must be fixed in the interim regardless of redesign timeline.

-   The homepage hero says only "Litigation Attorneys" with a generic boilerplate paragraph. There is no point of view, no representative case, no named attorney, no geography, no proof.

-   Practice area pages are thin and undifferentiated. Each reads like a single boilerplate paragraph. There are no representative matters, no attorney attribution, no jurisdiction-specific content, no FAQs, no internal links.

#### Strategic gaps

-   No local positioning. Stream Kim is in Riverside. The Inland Empire is a distinct, growing legal market. The current site never says "Riverside" or "Inland Empire" above the fold or in any meaningful structural way. Local SEO is being left on the table.

-   Attorneys are a black box from the homepage. A litigation firm is its lawyers. There are no named attorney features, no notable verdicts, no clerkships, no bar admissions, no media.

-   No proof or social signal. No Super Lawyers, Best Lawyers, AV Preeminent ratings; no notable matters; no client list (even anonymized); no media; no speaking engagements; no published articles.

-   No content marketing surface. No insights, no client alerts, no resource library. This is the largest long-term SEO opportunity the firm has — practice-specific content that ranks for substantive queries.

-   Weak conversion architecture. The CTA is a phone number and a contact form. There is no practice-area-specific intake, no consultation request flow, no "describe your matter" prompt calibrated to the institutional buyer.

#### Technical and accessibility issues

-   Practice area menu is alphabetical rather than ordered by strategic priority.

-   Footer practice list ordering does not match the menu.

-   Logo file appears small and pixelated on retina displays.

-   No visible LegalService, Attorney, or Organization schema markup.

-   ADA compliance status unknown. Over 3,500 ADA website lawsuits were filed in 2025; law firms are common targets. WCAG 2.1 AA compliance must be verified or remediated.

-   Core Web Vitals should be measured pre-redesign so improvement is provable post-launch.

### 3.4 What world-class looks like — distilled

Across the aspirational tier, three patterns repeat:

-   **Lawyers as the product.** Attorney bios are the most-trafficked pages on these sites. They lead with notable matters and clerkships, not headshots and feel-good copy.

## 4. Visual Direction & Brand System

The visual register must signal seriousness without slipping into stock-photo law-firm cliché (the conference-room handshake, the gavel, the law-book backdrop). Below is the proposed system. Final approval should come from the partners after a moodboard review, but this is the recommended direction.

### 4.1 Color palette

| | |
|---|---|
| Primary | Deep navy (#1F2A44 or similar) — institutional, trustworthy, restrained |
| Accent | Bordeaux / oxblood red (#8B0000 or similar) — used sparingly for emphasis, links, key CTAs. Already part of the existing 'Redbird' brand mark. |
| Neutrals | Warm off-white (#FAF8F5), mid-gray (#555555), charcoal (#222222) |
| Avoid | Bright tech-startup blues, gradients, drop shadows, glassmorphism, any aesthetic that signals 'modern' over 'serious' |

### 4.2 Typography

| | |
|---|---|
| Display / Headlines | A serif with editorial weight — recommend Tiempos Headline, Canela, or Source Serif Pro. Conveys authority and reading-room calm. |
| Body | A clean, highly legible sans-serif — recommend Inter, Söhne, or Source Sans 3. |
| Hierarchy | Generous size jumps, tight letter spacing on display, comfortable line-height (1.5–1.7) on body. |

### 4.3 Photography & imagery

Treat photography as a supporting element, not a decorative layer. The right approach:

-   Original attorney portraiture — neutral backdrop, natural light, professional but not retouched into uncanny territory. Black-and-white treatment optional and would suit the editorial register.

-   Architectural photography of the Riverside office and downtown Riverside — establishing the firm's geography is positioning, not filler.

-   No stock photography of gavels, scales of justice, marble columns, or generic professionals in suits. None.

-   Document and detail shots — close-ups of paper, pen, courtroom architecture — used as texture, not as the focal point.

### 4.4 Logo and identity

The existing 'Redbird' mark is acceptable and has equity worth preserving. Recommend:

-   Vector-based logo files at multiple sizes (the current logo is pixelated on retina).

-   A horizontal lockup for site header and a square mark for favicons / social profiles.

-   Refinement, not replacement. The mark itself is fine — the application is what needs work.

## 6. Page-by-Page Specifications

### 6.1 Homepage

Goal: Within ten seconds, a first-time visitor — likely a general counsel, public agency administrator, or referring attorney — should understand who Stream Kim is, what they do, where they do it, and that they operate at an institutional level.

#### Hero section

-   Headline: a short, declarative positioning statement. Recommended: "Civil trial counsel for institutions facing their hardest cases." Test against partner-approved alternatives.

-   Subhead: one sentence locating the firm geographically and identifying core practices. Example: "A Riverside civil litigation firm representing public entities, healthcare providers, construction-industry clients, and businesses across California."

-   Single primary CTA: 'Schedule a consultation' (links to a structured intake form, not a generic contact form).

-   Secondary CTA: phone number, click-to-call enabled.

-   No carousel. No hero video. A single, still architectural or attorney photograph, or a clean type-led hero with no image at all (Williams & Connolly does this).

#### Section 2: Practice areas

-   All nine practices, in strategic order, presented as a clean grid or stacked rows.

-   Each practice gets: practice name, one-sentence positioning line, link to the practice page.

-   No icons or illustrations unless extremely restrained and bespoke.

#### Section 3: Attorneys preview

-   Featured attorneys (3–5) with portrait, name, practice focus, and link to bio.

-   'View all attorneys' link to the index page.

#### Section 4: Notable matters / proof

-   3–5 representative matters, with ethics-compliant framing (e.g., "Defended a regional healthcare system in a multi-plaintiff EMTALA action" rather than identifying the client). California Rule of Professional Conduct 7.1–7.5 framing.

-   Ratings strip: AV Preeminent, Super Lawyers, Best Lawyers, Chambers, etc. — only the ones the firm actually holds. Verify before listing.

#### Section 5: Recent insights

-   3 most recent articles from the Insights section, with title, date, author, practice tag, and excerpt.

#### Section 6: Geographic positioning

-   A short statement of place: "Headquartered in Riverside, serving clients throughout the Inland Empire and across California." Office address, map, parking note for visitors.

#### Section 7: Contact / CTA closer

-   Final CTA with structured intake form.

-   Three intake fields minimum: name, email, brief description of matter. Phone optional. No more than five required fields.

### 6.2 Practice area page template

Each practice area page should run 800–1500 words and follow a consistent template. The current pages average a few hundred words and read as boilerplate. The new template:

15. Page hero: practice name, one-paragraph positioning statement specific to this practice.

16. Overview: 2–3 paragraphs describing the firm's approach to this practice, who they typically represent, and what kinds of matters.

17. Capabilities list: bulleted breakdown of the specific work the firm does within this practice (e.g. for Public Entity Defense: §1983 actions, Pitchess motions, Government Claims Act compliance, employment matters for public agencies, etc.).

18. Representative matters: 5–10 anonymized but specific case descriptions. This is the highest-credibility content on the page.

19. Attorneys in this practice: linked attorney cards with portrait, name, and one-line practice focus.

20. Related insights: 3 most recent articles tagged to this practice.

21. Jurisdiction & geography note: California-specific statutes, local court familiarity (Riverside Superior, San Bernardino Superior, Central District of California, etc.).

22. FAQ: 5–8 practice-specific questions written for SEO and for client education. Each answered in 2–4 sentences.

23. CTA: practice-specific consultation request.

### 6.3 Attorney bio template

Attorney pages will be among the most-trafficked on the site. Treat them as the product.

24. Portrait — professional, neutral background, consistent across all attorneys.

25. Name, title (Partner / Of Counsel / Associate), and contact line.

26. Lead paragraph: 2–3 sentences in the attorney's voice positioning their practice and what kind of clients they represent.

27. Notable matters: 5–10 representative cases this attorney has handled.

28. Practice areas: linked tags.

29. Bar admissions, court admissions, education (with years and honors).

30. Clerkships if applicable.

31. Publications & speaking engagements.

32. Honors & recognitions (Super Lawyers, Best Lawyers, etc.).

33. Professional and community memberships.

34. Direct contact form / vCard download.

### 6.4 Insights (blog) template

-   Index page: filter by practice, sort by date. Clean editorial layout — title, excerpt, author, date, tag.

-   Individual article: clean reading width (~700px), large body type, author byline with portrait and link to bio, related articles, practice area tags.

-   Newsletter signup at end of each article.

-   Schema markup: Article + Person (author) for SEO.

### 6.5 Notable Matters page

-   Filterable by practice area.

-   Each matter: short title, 2–4 sentence description, ethics-compliant (no client identification unless explicitly authorized), tags.

-   Acts as both a credibility surface and an SEO surface for substantive practice queries.

### 6.6 Contact page

-   Structured intake form — name, email, phone (optional), practice area dropdown, brief matter description.

-   Office address, phone, fax, email, embedded map.

-   Office hours, parking notes, accessibility notes.

-   Required ethical disclaimer: 'Submission of this form does not create an attorney-client relationship.'

## 7. Content Strategy

### 7.1 Editorial voice

The voice is institutional, confident, restrained. It assumes the reader is sophisticated. It does not over-explain, over-promise, or use marketing-speak.

-   **Use:** "We represent public entities in their most sensitive matters," "Stream Kim has tried over [X] cases to verdict in California state and federal courts," "Our healthcare practice advises hospital systems and ambulance providers on regulatory and litigation matters."

-   **Avoid:** "Premier," "award-winning," "#1," "trust," "passionate," "family," exclamation points, sales-page CTAs.

### 7.2 Content production plan

Treat the Insights section as a sustained business-development asset, not a vanity blog. Recommend a publication cadence the firm can sustain:

-   2 articles per month at launch, scaling to 4 per month within 6 months.

-   Each article authored by an attorney and 600–1200 words.

-   Topic mix: 60% practice-specific legal analysis (e.g., "California's new Senate Bill XYZ and what it means for public agencies"), 25% case-law commentary, 15% firm news / speaking engagements / community involvement.

-   Editorial calendar maintained quarterly. Each attorney commits to 4–6 articles per year.

### 7.3 Initial launch content checklist

Before launch, the following must be ready:

-   9 practice area pages, each 800+ words, with representative matters and FAQs.

-   Attorney bio for every attorney, following the new template.

-   12–15 Insights articles to populate the blog at launch (avoid empty-blog appearance).

-   20–30 representative matters across all practices.

-   Updated firm overview / About copy.

-   All required legal disclaimers and privacy/terms pages.

## 8. SEO, Technical, and Compliance

### 8.1 SEO foundation

-   Single domain, no subdomains. Each practice area is a /practice-area-name/ subdirectory.

-   Title tags and meta descriptions written individually for every page — no theme defaults.

-   Schema markup: Organization, LegalService (per practice), Attorney (per bio), Article (per insight), LocalBusiness, BreadcrumbList.

-   XML sitemap, robots.txt, canonical tags.

-   Internal linking discipline: every practice page links to relevant attorneys and at least 3 insights; every attorney page links to their practices and authored insights; every insight links back to relevant practice and attorney.

-   Local SEO: claim and optimize Google Business Profile, ensure NAP (name/address/phone) consistency across the site and across all directory listings (Avvo, Justia, Martindale, Super Lawyers, Best Lawyers, FindLaw, local bar association).

### 8.2 Technical stack recommendations

Recommendation depends on Stream Kim's internal capacity and ongoing maintenance preferences. Two viable paths:

| | |
|---|---|
| Path A: WordPress (managed) | Familiar to legal industry, easier handoff to non-technical staff, broad agency support. Use a clean custom theme — not a theme marketplace template. Hosted on a managed provider (WP Engine, Kinsta). Estimated annual hosting: $300--$1,200. |
| Path B: Headless (Next.js + CMS) | Faster, more secure, better Core Web Vitals, more flexible. CMS options: Sanity, Contentful, or Payload. Higher build cost, lower long-term technical debt, requires a developer relationship for changes beyond content edits. Recommended if Halliday will continue maintaining the site. |

Recommendation: Path B (headless Next.js + Sanity or Payload), given Halliday's existing Next.js / Supabase / Vercel skill set and the long-term maintenance relationship.

### 8.3 Performance targets

-   Core Web Vitals: LCP < 2.0s, INP < 200ms, CLS < 0.05 on mobile and desktop.

-   Lighthouse score: 95+ across Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices, SEO.

-   Page weight: < 1.5MB on initial load for content pages.

-   Time to Interactive: < 2.5s on 4G mobile.

### 8.4 Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA)

-   Color contrast 4.5:1 minimum for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI.

-   Full keyboard navigation; visible focus states.

-   Semantic HTML; landmark roles.

-   Alt text for all meaningful images; null alt for decorative.

-   Form labels properly associated; error states announced to screen readers.

-   Pre-launch audit by a third-party accessibility tool (axe DevTools, WAVE) plus manual screen reader test.

-   Accessibility statement page.

### 8.5 Legal & ethical compliance

-   California Rule of Professional Conduct 7.1 (communications about legal services): no false or misleading statements. Every credibility claim must be substantiated.

-   CRPC 7.2 (advertising): all client testimonials and case results must include disclaimers.

-   Privacy policy compliant with CCPA / CPRA (the firm is California-based and serves California clients).

-   Cookie banner if any third-party tracking is used.

-   ADA compliance (above) is also a litigation-risk control.

-   Required disclaimer on every page: 'The information on this website is for general informational purposes only. Nothing on this site should be taken as legal advice for any individual case or situation.'

### 8.6 Analytics & measurement

-   Google Analytics 4 with goal tracking on consultation form submissions, phone clicks, email clicks, and PDF downloads.

-   Google Search Console connected pre-launch.

-   Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar for session recording (with privacy notice update).

-   Quarterly performance review: organic traffic, top entry pages, conversion rate by traffic source, ranking position for target keywords.

## 9. Project Plan & Phasing

### 9.1 Phased build

Recommend a 12–14 week build, split into four phases. The phasing prioritizes fixing the broken stat block and obvious credibility defects in week 1, regardless of the redesign timeline.

#### Phase 0 — Triage (Week 1)

-   Fix or remove broken stat block on current site.

-   Add explicit Riverside / Inland Empire language to current homepage.

-   Patch any obvious accessibility issues that create legal exposure.

#### Phase 1 — Discovery & Strategy (Weeks 2–3)

-   Stakeholder interviews with all partners (positioning, voice, what kinds of clients they want more of).

-   Attorney interviews (2 per attorney, ~45 minutes each) to source notable matters and bio content.

-   Review of historical client list, matter list, recognitions to source proof content.

-   Final positioning, sitemap, and content inventory locked.

#### Phase 2 — Design (Weeks 4–6)

-   Moodboard review and approval.

-   Homepage and one practice area page designed in Figma to high fidelity.

-   Design system (color, type, spacing, components) documented.

-   Attorney bio template, insights template, contact template.

-   Mobile and desktop versions for all templates.

#### Phase 3 — Build & Content (Weeks 5–11, overlapping)

-   Front-end build (Next.js), CMS setup, content modeling.

-   Practice area pages drafted, reviewed, revised — partner sign-off on each.

-   Attorney bios drafted from interview transcripts, revised, signed off.

-   Insights launch content — 12–15 articles drafted across attorneys.

-   Notable matters compiled and ethics-reviewed.

-   Photography shoot for attorney portraits and office establishing shots.

#### Phase 4 — QA & Launch (Weeks 12–14)

-   Cross-browser testing (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge — desktop and mobile).

-   Accessibility audit and remediation.

-   Performance audit (Core Web Vitals).

-   Final ethical review of all client-facing copy by partners.

-   301 redirect map from old URLs to new (preserve any inbound link equity).

-   Search Console & Analytics set up; baseline metrics captured.

-   Soft launch, partner review, public launch.
