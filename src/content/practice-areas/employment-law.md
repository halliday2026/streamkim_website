faqItems:
  - question: "What is PAGA, and how does it differ from a class action?"
    answer: >
      California's Private Attorneys General Act (Labor Code § 2698 et seq.) allows
      an aggrieved employee to bring a civil action on behalf of the state to recover
      civil penalties for Labor Code violations. Unlike a class action, PAGA does not
      require class certification, and a significant portion of recovered penalties
      goes to the Labor and Workforce Development Agency rather than to the employees.
      PAGA claims are often brought alongside wage-and-hour class actions and present
      distinct strategic considerations for employers.
  - question: "What agencies can enforce California employment law against employers?"
    answer: >
      California employers face enforcement from multiple state and federal agencies.
      The Civil Rights Department handles discrimination, harassment, and retaliation
      complaints. Cal/OSHA enforces workplace safety standards. The Labor Commissioner
      handles wage claims and retaliation matters. The Workers' Compensation Appeals
      Board handles workers' compensation disputes. Federally, the EEOC enforces
      Title VII and other federal anti-discrimination laws, and the Department of
      Labor enforces federal wage and hour standards. Stream Kim represents employers
      before all of these agencies.
  - question: "What is the Cal-WARN Act, and when does it apply?"
    answer: >
      California's WARN Act (Labor Code § 1400 et seq.) requires employers with 75 or
      more full- and part-time employees to provide 60 days' advance notice before
      ordering a mass layoff, relocation, or termination of covered employees.
      California's law is broader than the federal WARN Act in several respects,
      including its coverage threshold and the definition of qualifying events. Failure
      to provide proper notice can result in liability for back pay and benefits for
      each affected employee for up to 60 days.

## California Employment Law for Employers

California employment law generates more litigation per employer than virtually any other state's framework. Wage-and-hour class actions, PAGA representative actions, harassment and discrimination claims, and agency enforcement proceedings are a routine cost of doing business in California — managed, not eliminated, by employers who maintain strong employment practices and skilled outside counsel.

Stream Kim's employment law practice is structured around employer-side representation. The firm's employment attorneys have spent more than two decades representing California employers in every significant forum where employment disputes arise: California superior courts, federal district courts, and the full range of state and federal agencies with employment law jurisdiction. That breadth of experience informs both the firm's litigation strategy and its compliance advice.

The firm represents employers across industries, from large regional employers in the Inland Empire to businesses with statewide operations. Its employment attorneys advise clients on wage-and-hour compliance, handbook and policy development, and investigation procedures as part of an integrated practice that connects preventive counseling to courtroom representation.

## PAGA and Class Action Defense

PAGA litigation has become one of the most significant sources of employment law exposure for California employers. Stream Kim's employment attorneys are experienced in defending PAGA representative actions and wage-and-hour class actions — including managing the intersection of the two when they are brought together — and in negotiating resolutions that appropriately account for the firm's employer clients' interests.
