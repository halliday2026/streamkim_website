  - question: "What is the Government Claims Act, and when does it apply?"
    answer: >
      California's Government Claims Act (Government Code § 810 et seq.) requires
      anyone seeking money damages from a public entity to present a written claim to
      the entity before filing a lawsuit. For personal injury, wrongful death, and
      property damage claims, this claim must generally be filed within six months of
      the date of the incident. Failure to timely present a claim is a complete defense
      in most cases, and public agencies should respond to every claim with that
      procedural posture in mind.
  - question: "What is qualified immunity, and does it apply in California?"
    answer: >
      Qualified immunity is a federal doctrine that protects government officials from
      personal liability under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 unless they violated a clearly
      established constitutional right. California has its own statutory immunity
      provisions for public entities and public employees under the Government Claims
      Act. The interplay between federal and state immunity doctrines is a significant
      element of public entity defense strategy in civil rights cases.
  - question: "How should a public agency respond when it receives a government tort claim?"
    answer: >
      When a public agency receives a government tort claim, it generally has 45 days
      to accept, reject, or return the claim as insufficient. A thoughtful response —
      informed by a prompt investigation — can significantly affect the scope and
      direction of subsequent litigation. Stream Kim advises public agencies on claims
      response strategy as part of its overall public entity defense representation.

## Public Entity Litigation in California

California public agencies operate within a specialized litigation environment that requires counsel familiar with both constitutional law and the procedural requirements that govern when and how public entities can be sued. Stream Kim's public entity defense practice reflects decades of experience representing cities, counties, school districts, special districts, and other government bodies across the state.

The firm's public entity work spans the core categories of government liability: § 1983 civil rights actions, tort claims arising from public services and infrastructure, employment disputes involving public agency employees, and land use controversies. Each of these areas requires familiarity not only with the substantive law but also with the Government Claims Act's procedural requirements, which shape pre-litigation strategy and define the agency's available defenses.

Stream Kim is based in Riverside, giving our attorneys day-to-day familiarity with the courts and agencies of the Inland Empire. Riverside County and San Bernardino County together constitute one of California's most populous regions, and the firm's public agency clients include large municipal governments and smaller special districts throughout the area.

## The Stakes of Public Agency Litigation

Public entity litigation carries institutional stakes that differ from private civil litigation. A judgment against a public agency affects budgets, insurance premiums, and public confidence in government operations. Matters involving law enforcement, school districts, and civil rights draw scrutiny beyond the courtroom. Stream Kim's approach to public entity defense reflects an understanding of those broader stakes and an orientation toward outcomes that protect the agency's long-term institutional interests as well as the immediate matter at hand.
