faqItems:
  - question: "What is the standard of review, and why does it matter on appeal?"
    answer: >
      The standard of review determines how much deference an appellate court gives
      to the trial court's rulings. Questions of law are reviewed de novo — the
      appellate court forms its own judgment without deference to the trial court.
      Factual findings are reviewed for substantial evidence, meaning the appellate
      court will affirm if any reasonable finder of fact could have reached the same
      conclusion. Discretionary rulings are reviewed for abuse of discretion.
      Appellate strategy must account for the applicable standard at each step.
  - question: "When should a client consider pursuing an appeal?"
    answer: >
      An appeal should be considered when there is a genuine legal error in the trial
      court's ruling — not merely a disappointing result — and when the error affected
      the outcome. Because appeals are decided on the record developed at trial, the
      strength of an appeal is largely determined by what happened in the trial court,
      including how objections were preserved. Early consultation with appellate
      counsel, even before judgment is entered, can preserve issues and improve
      the chances of a successful appeal.
  - question: "What is writ practice, and when is it appropriate?"
    answer: >
      Writ practice involves petitioning an appellate court to review a trial court
      ruling before the case concludes — an extraordinary remedy available in limited
      circumstances. Writs of mandate and prohibition are the most common vehicles,
      used when a trial court has acted in excess of its jurisdiction or failed to
      perform a ministerial duty. Because most interlocutory orders are not immediately
      appealable, writ practice is sometimes the only way to obtain pre-judgment
      appellate review of a ruling that could significantly affect the outcome of
      the case.

## Appellate Practice

Appellate advocacy is a specialized discipline. The skills required to persuade a trial court — witness examination, evidentiary objections, courtroom presence — give way at the appellate level to the written brief and the oral argument before a panel of judges who come to the case with the full record before them. Stream Kim's appellate lawyers bring the analytical rigor and writing precision that effective appellate advocacy requires.

The firm's appellate work encompasses both California state court appeals and federal circuit appeals. In state court, matters from the Inland Empire are assigned to the Fourth Appellate District, Division Two, in Riverside — a court with which Stream Kim attorneys have regular familiarity. Federal appeals from Stream Kim's clients proceed to the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals.

Stream Kim also advises trial teams on preserving appellate issues during trial court proceedings. The best appellate outcomes begin with proper issue preservation at the trial level — a connection that makes the integration of appellate awareness into trial strategy an important element of effective representation.

## Writ and Extraordinary Relief

In some circumstances, waiting for a final judgment to appeal is not a viable option. Stream Kim's appellate attorneys handle writ petitions and other extraordinary relief applications when immediate appellate review is warranted — including applications challenging trial court evidentiary rulings, jurisdictional decisions, and discovery orders that would cause irreparable harm if not reviewed before judgment.
