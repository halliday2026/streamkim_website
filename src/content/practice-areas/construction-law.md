  - question: "What is the Right to Repair Act, and how does it affect construction defect claims?"
    answer: >
      California's Right to Repair Act (SB 800, Civil Code §§ 895–945.5) applies to
      newly constructed residential units and establishes specific functionality
      standards that construction must meet. Before filing suit under the Act, a
      homeowner must generally give the builder notice and an opportunity to inspect
      and offer repairs. The Act also establishes specific limitations periods for
      different categories of defects. Stream Kim advises contractors and developers
      on Right to Repair Act compliance and defends them in claims arising under
      the statute.
  - question: "What is the difference between a patent and a latent construction defect?"
    answer: >
      A patent defect is one that is visible and discoverable upon reasonable
      inspection; a latent defect is hidden and not reasonably discoverable without
      investigation. This distinction matters because different statutes of limitations
      apply: patent defects are generally subject to a four-year limitations period
      under Code of Civil Procedure § 337.1, while latent defects are governed by the
      ten-year statute of repose in § 337.15. The discovery rule may also affect when
      the limitations period begins to run for latent defects.
  - question: "What is a mechanic's lien, and how can contractors protect their lien rights?"
    answer: >
      A mechanic's lien is a statutory security interest that contractors,
      subcontractors, and suppliers can place on property to secure payment for work
      performed or materials furnished. California's mechanic's lien statutes impose
      strict procedural requirements, including preliminary notice obligations and
      deadlines for recording the lien and filing suit to enforce it. Contractors and
      subcontractors should work with counsel to ensure their lien rights are properly
      preserved on every project.

## Construction Law in California

California construction law encompasses a broad range of statutory and common law claims that arise at every stage of a project — from contract formation through completion, defect claims, and final payment disputes. Stream Kim's construction law practice reflects the firm's experience representing the parties who bear the most legal exposure in California construction projects: general contractors, specialty subcontractors, developers, and design professionals.

The firm's construction work includes both litigation and transactional matters. On the litigation side, Stream Kim handles construction defect claims, delay and impact disputes, contract termination controversies, and lien and bond claims. On the transactional side, the firm reviews and drafts construction contracts, advises on risk allocation, and helps clients structure project agreements to reduce their litigation exposure.

The Inland Empire is a region of substantial construction activity, and Stream Kim's Riverside base places the firm in proximity to the developers, contractors, and property owners who are its construction clients. The firm's attorneys are familiar with the courts, practices, and project types that characterize construction work in Riverside and San Bernardino Counties.

## California's Construction Defect Framework

California construction defect litigation operates under a framework that includes the Right to Repair Act for residential construction, the patent and latent defect statutes of limitations, and the discovery rule that can extend when the limitations period begins to run. Understanding this framework — and structuring defense strategy accordingly — is a core element of Stream Kim's construction practice.
