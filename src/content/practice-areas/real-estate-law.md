faqItems:
  - question: "What is a partition action, and when is it used?"
    answer: >
      A partition action is a legal proceeding to divide jointly owned real property
      among co-owners who cannot agree on how to use or sell the property. California
      courts can order physical division of the property where feasible, or — more
      commonly for commercial properties — order a sale and division of the proceeds.
      Partition actions are frequently used to resolve disputes among co-owners of
      inherited property, investment properties, and commercial real estate where
      the co-ownership relationship has broken down.
  - question: "What obligations do real estate brokers and agents have in California?"
    answer: >
      California real estate brokers and agents owe fiduciary duties to their
      principals, including duties of undivided loyalty, reasonable care, disclosure
      of material facts, and accounting. Breach of these duties can give rise to
      liability in civil litigation. Stream Kim defends real estate professionals
      facing claims arising from transactions, including allegations of failure to
      disclose, negligent misrepresentation, and breach of fiduciary duty.
  - question: "What is Proposition 8, and how do property owners challenge assessments under it?"
    answer: >
      Proposition 8, passed in 1978, allows county assessors to temporarily reduce
      a property's assessed value when its current market value falls below the
      Proposition 13 factored base year value. When market values recover, the
      assessor may restore the assessed value up to the Proposition 13 base year
      value. Property owners who believe their assessed value is excessive can file
      an assessment appeal with the county Assessment Appeals Board. Stream Kim has
      represented property owners in Proposition 8 and Proposition 13 assessment
      disputes.

## Real Property Litigation in California

California's real estate market is one of the country's most active, and the litigation it generates reflects that activity. From commercial property disputes and lender-borrower controversies to title claims and broker liability matters, Stream Kim's real estate practice addresses the full range of civil disputes involving California real property.

The firm represents property owners, developers, investors, brokers, agents, and lenders. Its real estate attorneys bring both litigation depth and transactional familiarity to these matters — understanding contract terms, title insurance, escrow mechanics, and financing structures that are relevant to the disputes the firm handles.

The Inland Empire's real estate market has experienced significant activity over the past decade, with both commercial and residential development concentrated in Riverside and San Bernardino Counties. Stream Kim's Riverside base gives the firm day-to-day involvement in the regional market's legal disputes, including the development controversies, infrastructure disputes, and property ownership conflicts that arise in the region.

## Property Valuation and Tax Disputes

Among the more specialized matters Stream Kim handles in its real estate practice are property tax assessment disputes arising under Propositions 8 and 13. These disputes require familiarity with California's property tax assessment framework, the Assessment Appeals Board process, and the valuation methodologies that govern challenged assessments. The firm has represented property owners in these proceedings as part of its broader real estate dispute practice.
