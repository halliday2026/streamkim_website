  - question: "Are non-compete agreements enforceable in California?"
    answer: >
      California Business and Professions Code § 16600 broadly prohibits contractual
      restrictions on an employee's ability to engage in a profession or trade after
      leaving an employer. Most non-compete agreements are unenforceable in California,
      with limited statutory exceptions for the sale of a business or dissolution of
      a partnership. However, non-solicitation of customers and confidentiality
      agreements may be enforceable in appropriate circumstances. The enforceability
      of these provisions is a frequently litigated question, and employers and
      employees alike benefit from counsel familiar with California's specific rules.
  - question: "What qualifies as a trade secret under California law?"
    answer: >
      Under the California Uniform Trade Secrets Act (Civil Code § 3426 et seq.),
      a trade secret is information that derives independent economic value from not
      being generally known or readily ascertainable, and that is the subject of
      reasonable efforts to maintain its secrecy. Customer lists, pricing information,
      formulas, processes, and business strategies can all qualify as trade secrets
      if they meet this standard. Businesses must take affirmative steps to protect
      their trade secrets — through confidentiality agreements, access controls, and
      security protocols — to maintain their legal protections.
  - question: "What remedies are available for trade secret misappropriation?"
    answer: >
      California law provides both injunctive relief and damages for trade secret
      misappropriation. Courts can enjoin the actual or threatened misappropriation
      of a trade secret and order affirmative acts to protect the trade secret.
      Damages may include the actual loss caused by the misappropriation, the unjust
      enrichment of the misappropriating party, and, in cases of willful and
      malicious misappropriation, exemplary damages of up to twice the actual
      damages. Attorney's fees may also be available in appropriate cases.

## Trade Secrets and Intellectual Property Litigation

California's business community faces a distinctive intellectual property landscape. Unlike the patent and copyright matters that dominate federal IP practice, most IP litigation affecting California businesses involves trade secrets, confidentiality obligations, and the boundaries of competitive conduct. Stream Kim's IP practice focuses on these areas — the categories of IP law that most frequently affect the firm's business clients.

Trade secret litigation arises when confidential business information is misappropriated — most often by departing employees or competitors who have gained improper access. These matters move quickly: the most effective trade secret litigation begins with a prompt assessment of what information is at stake, who has it, and whether emergency injunctive relief is warranted. Stream Kim's attorneys are experienced in securing temporary restraining orders and preliminary injunctions in trade secret matters when the circumstances warrant.

California's broad prohibition on non-compete agreements (Business and Professions Code § 16600) creates a distinctive environment for employment-related IP disputes. While non-competes are largely unenforceable, other forms of employee agreements — confidentiality agreements, non-solicitation provisions, and invention assignment agreements — remain significant legal tools. The firm advises employers on structuring these agreements and defends them in disputes over their scope and enforceability.

## Federal Trade Secret Protection

The federal Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA) provides an additional avenue for trade secret protection in federal court and allows for civil seizure orders in extraordinary circumstances. Stream Kim advises clients on whether to bring trade secret claims in California state court under CUTSA, in federal court under the DTSA, or in both, based on the specific facts and strategic objectives of each matter.
