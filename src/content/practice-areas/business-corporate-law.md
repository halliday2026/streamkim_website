faqItems:
  - question: "What is the difference between a breach of contract claim and a business tort claim?"
    answer: >
      A breach of contract claim arises from a failure to perform a contractual
      obligation and generally limits recovery to economic damages flowing from the
      breach. A business tort claim — such as fraud, negligent misrepresentation,
      or intentional interference with contractual relations — arises from a duty
      independent of contract and may support additional categories of damages,
      including punitive damages in appropriate cases. Both types of claims frequently
      arise from the same business dispute, and counsel must evaluate which theory
      best serves the client's interests.
  - question: "What fiduciary duties do partners and shareholders owe one another in California?"
    answer: >
      California law imposes fiduciary duties of loyalty, care, and good faith on
      general partners, managing members of LLCs, and, in some circumstances,
      majority shareholders. These duties prohibit self-dealing, usurpation of
      corporate opportunities, and conduct that prioritizes individual interests
      over those of the business entity. Breach of fiduciary duty claims are a
      common feature of partnership dissolution disputes and shareholder controversies.
  - question: "What remedies are available when a business partner breaches the partnership agreement?"
    answer: >
      When a business partner breaches the partnership agreement, available remedies
      may include damages for the economic harm caused by the breach, specific
      performance of the agreement's terms, an accounting of partnership assets,
      and — in cases of serious misconduct — judicial dissolution of the partnership.
      The availability and advisability of each remedy depends on the specific terms
      of the agreement, the nature of the breach, and the practical situation of
      the remaining partners. Early consultation with litigation counsel can help
      preserve the full range of available options.

## Business Litigation in California

California's commercial environment generates a substantial volume of business litigation, much of it arising from the complexity of California's business law framework and the state's active transaction market. Stream Kim's business and corporate law practice is built around experienced trial lawyers who understand the commercial context behind business disputes and the stakes involved for companies and their owners.

The firm's business lawyers represent clients in commercial contract disputes, partnership and shareholder controversies, business tort claims, and fraud litigation. They also advise on corporate governance questions and business formation issues that have litigation implications — the kind of structural work that reduces the risk of future disputes.

Stream Kim's business clients range from small and mid-size Inland Empire businesses to larger companies with California operations. The firm's Riverside base gives it proximity to the regional business community while its attorneys' experience extends to commercial litigation throughout Southern California.

## Franchise and Commercial Lease Disputes

Among the more specialized business litigation matters Stream Kim handles are franchise agreement disputes and commercial lease controversies. Franchise litigation involves the intersection of contract law, intellectual property, and California's Franchise Investment Law, while commercial lease disputes frequently raise issues of landlord remedies, tenant defenses, and the valuation of lease obligations. The firm has represented both franchisors and franchisees, and both landlords and tenants, in these categories of commercial disputes.
